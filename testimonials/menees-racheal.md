Provided by the WKU social media coordinator from a CEBS Student Ambassador Q&A.

Why do you want to be a teacher?

> I want to be a teacher because I enjoy getting the chance to mentor others. I absolutely love hanging out with the students when I’m at the school for class or when I was working at an after-school program. The students would make my day so much brighter and I would get to hear all their fun stories and just be a person they could come to if they were sad or struggling. I also want to be a teacher because I’m lifelong learner which means to me that I am a person who wants to continue learning throughout their life, not just in the K-12 classroom. I always enjoy what I learn in classes, especially when I got to college and started taking general education classes. Though I’m not in my major specific classes, I still have so much fun gaining knowledge about other subjects like history, American Sign Language, civics, and more. I am so excited to take what I have learned in my own courses and teach my future students the same things!

How is WKU preparing you for your future classroom?

> WKU is preparing me for my future classroom by putting me in the classrooms at such an early time in my career. In my freshman year I took EDU 250 and EDU 350 where I was required to get 30 hours of observations total for that semester. WKU even set all of that up where I could easily get my first 10 hours, and then they gave me chances to get the other 20 of my hours. After doing observations for 2, almost 3 semesters so far, I already feel confident in my presence inside the classroom as a teacher. My first observation I didn’t want to help the students with their classwork because I didn’t want to get anything wrong and I wasn’t comfortable with guiding the students to their answers yet. Now, in my 3rd semester of observations, I was able to guide students on tests! I certainly did not give anyone answers but more so read aloud questions and pointed out things like “it says the answer is in paragraph 2” because my mentor teacher had already pointed that out before they continued their test. Those things I would not have felt comfortable doing 1 year ago and so my confidence from observations that WKU helps in getting done has helped me so much!
>
> Of course, our courses also help us. PSY 310 taught me a lot about how different age groups brains work, which was extremely helpful when I was working at the after-school program in Warren County because I worked with students in kindergarten all the way to 5th grade. My EDU courses (250, 260, 350, and 360) have also prepared me for creating behavioral plans, good assessments, and being a teacher in a diverse community are all things that I have learned in my courses.
