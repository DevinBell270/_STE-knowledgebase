## One Pathway.   
Multiple Credentials.

**Maximize the Same 30 Hours.** Don't spend 30 hours on a rank-only certificate. WKU’s stackable graduate pathways let Kentucky teachers earn a master's degree, advance their rank, and add high-demand endorsements simultaneously.

### The WKU Advantage

#### Same Effort, 5x the Credentials

Whether advancing to Rank II or Rank I, you need 30 graduate hours. Don't spend them on a rank-only certificate that limits your career growth. WKU’s stackable MAE, MS, and EdS pathways are intentionally engineered to build a career-defining, multi-credential portfolio.

#### Stackable MAE / MS / EdS

  * **1\. Full Master's Degree:** Conferred MAE or MS 
  * **2\. Kentucky Rank Advancement:** Rank II or Rank I 
  * **3\. State Endorsements:** Gifted, Literacy, ESL, etc. 
  * **4\. Dual Credit Eligibility:** 18+ content graduate hours 
  * **5\. Nationwide Portability:** Recognized across all 50 states 



Maximum return on investment for the same 30 credit hours.

#### *** Accreditation and reciprocity**

WKU master’s degrees are SACSCOC and CAEP accredited and recognized across all 50 states for advanced salary placement (Master’s / Master’s + 30). Kentucky participates in the NASDTEC reciprocity agreement with 45+ states. The degree and salary status transfer nationwide; receiving state boards may still require local testing, background checks, or state-specific modules for license transfer.

### How Stacking Works

Stacking is course planning, not extra hours. You choose a 30-hour MAE, MS, or EdS, then align the core, concentration, and electives so rank change, endorsements, and dual-credit hours land in the same program.

The Base

Choose a 30-Hour Degree & Partner with an Advisor

Start with an approved MAE, MS, or EdS program. Your advisor works with you from day one to strategically map your elective and concentration slots so every credit hour works toward multiple credentials.

Add-On

Embed certificates and endorsements

Use concentration and elective hours for high-demand credentials such as Gifted Education, TESOL, Literacy, or Instructional Design instead of generic rank-only coursework.

Add-On

Advance to Rank II or Rank I

The same 30 hours support permanent district salary advancement from Rank III to Rank II, or Rank II to Rank I, with no extra coursework required.

Add-On

Add dual-credit eligibility

Allocate 18 of your 30 graduate hours to a specific content discipline (such as English, mathematics, or science) to qualify to teach college-level dual-credit courses in your high school.

### Degrees, Certificates, and Endorsements

Browse the graduate degrees, certificates, endorsements, and proficiency routes that can share one 30-hour plan. An advisor will confirm which combinations fit your current certificate and district goals.

Master’s and Specialist Degree Options (30 Hours)

  * **Advanced Teacher Education, MAE (0500):** Customizable pathway pairing graduate content with certificates and endorsements.
  * **Gifted Education and Talent Development, MAE (0482):** Specialized leadership in gifted pedagogy and programming.
  * **Instructional Design, MS (0428):** Training design, educational technology, and multimedia development.
  * **Interdisciplinary Early Childhood Education (IECE), Birth to Primary, MAE (0461):** Birth through primary specialist preparation.
  * **Libraries, Informatics, and Technology in Education (LITE), MS (0497):** School librarian certification and digital coaching.
  * **Literacy Education, MAE (044):** Comprehensive reading specialist preparation.
  * **Special Education: Learning and Behavior Disorders (LBD), MAE (0457):** Initial or advanced LBD P-12 teaching certification.
  * **Special Education: Moderate and Severe Disabilities (MSD), MAE (0438):** Specialized low-incidence disability instruction.
  * **Educational Specialist Degree (EDS 0503):** Post-master’s Rank I advancement with an applied research focus.

Secondary teachers completing 18 graduate hours in a specific content area (such as English, mathematics, or science) may qualify to teach dual-credit courses in their high schools.
