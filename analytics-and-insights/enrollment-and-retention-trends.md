### Key Recruitment Funnel Takeaways
1.  **Strong Pipeline Growth (+26.4% in 2 Years):** The total number of incoming freshmen registering for orientation in teaching programs expanded from **140 in Fall 2024 to 157 in Fall 2025 and 177 in Fall 2026**.
2.  **Elementary Education Dominance:** Elementary Education (#527) grew by **18 students (+18.6%)** in 2026, reaching **115 incoming freshmen** and accounting for **65.0% of all incoming teacher education freshmen**.
3.  **Surge in Early Childhood:** Interdisciplinary Early Childhood Education (#526) saw a **+266% expansion**, leaping from 3 incoming students in 2025 to **11 in 2026**, reflecting growing regional investment in early childhood intervention.
4.  **Increasing Share of University Enrollment:** Teaching majors account for **7.3% of the entire university's net orientation cohort** (177 out of 2,426 students), up from 6.7% in 2025 and 5.5% in 2024.

## Strategic Implications & Takeaways for Agents & Advisors

1.  **Explosive Growth in Initial Certification MAT (#0495):**
    Enrollment in MAT 0495 jumped from **9 students in Fall 2022** to **85 in Fall 2023, 86 in Fall 2024, 84 in Fall 2025, and 91 in Fall 2026**. This 10x surge demonstrates the massive impact of the [[pathways-and-certification/alternative-certification-option-6|Option 6 Alternative Certification route]] as school districts actively hire bachelor's graduates directly into paid teaching roles.
2.  **Rapid Expansion of the Stackable MAE (#0500):**
    The MAE in Advanced Teacher Education grew steadily from **19 students in Fall 2022 to 42 students in Fall 2026 (+121%)**, confirming that practicing educators strongly prefer the stackable 30-hour master's path over standalone rank certificates.
3.  **Resilient Dual Certification Special Education Demand:**
    The undergraduate dual major in SPED LBD & Elementary Education (#5003) maintains a large cohort (109 students in Fall 2026) and boasts high retention rates (**85.7%** in Fall 2025, **88.0%** in Fall 2023).
4.  **Equity Focus in First-Generation Retention:**
    The demographic data indicates an ~18 percentage point retention gap between first-generation (65.9%) and continuing-generation (84.0%) students in CEBS, underscoring the critical need for early advising, peer mentoring, and [[funding-and-aid/teacher-funding-guide|stackable financial aid counseling]].
