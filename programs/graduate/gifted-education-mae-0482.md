# Gifted Education and Talent Development, MAE (Ref: 0482)

*School of Teacher Education*  
*College of Education and Behavioral Sciences*  
*Western Kentucky University*

The **Master of Arts in Education in Gifted Education and Talent Development (Reference # 0482)** is an internationally recognized graduate degree housed in WKU's endowed Center for Gifted Studies. Led by [[people/dr-julia-link-roberts|Dr. Julia Link Roberts]], the program prepares educators to identify, nurture, and challenge advanced, creative, and gifted learners across **Grades P through 12**.

Leads to the **Kentucky Gifted and Talented Endorsement (KE37, Grades P–12)** for eligible certified teachers and qualifies for the **$350/credit hour [[funding-and-aid/educator-tuition-discount|WKU Graduate Educator Tuition Discount]]**.

The MAE with an **Advanced Instruction** focus has two components: an **18-hour gifted education required sequence** (with **GTE 538** as the research practicum) and a **12–16 hour concentration**. Candidates who complete the MAE will be eligible for a recommendation for **Rank II** with Gifted and Talented Education (KE37) endorsement, WKU Gifted and Talented certificate, and any other corresponding certificates/endorsements. Individuals holding a Rank II will be eligible for a **Rank I** with KE37 endorsement and corresponding credentials. An additional three-hour practicum and a passing score on Praxis II in Gifted Education are required for those seeking the endorsement in gifted education.

The **Advanced Research** concentration may be used for Rank II or Rank I and can prepare students for careers in higher education, government, policy work, or other organizations (including pathways for educators seeking a rank change, educators seeking rank change plus the gifted endorsement, and individuals who are not certified personnel).

The MAE is offered primarily online. Exceptions include the practicum and requests for face-to-face instruction for cohort groups. This program is exempt from the maximum of 12 hours denoted in the Duplication of Coursework policy. All concentration hours may be duplicated on one additional graduate degree or certificate at the same level. Concentrations that also lead to a certificate require a separate application for admission to the corresponding certificate and/or certification/endorsement.

## Admission Requirements
Criteria vary by undergraduate institution, GPA, and concentration. For the Advanced Instruction pathway, a copy of the applicant’s teaching certificate or statement of eligibility must be submitted.

*   **WKU teacher-preparation alumni:** current, valid teaching certificate or Statement of Eligibility for IECE Birth to Primary; Elementary P-5; Middle Grades 5-9; Secondary 8-12, 5-12, or P-12; or Special Education P-12.
*   **Graduate of a Kentucky institution other than WKU, or an out-of-state accredited university:** 2.75 GPA on all previous undergraduate coursework and 3.0 GPA on previous graduate coursework (or qualifying GAP scores), and must have or be eligible for a teaching certificate in one of the areas listed above.

Kentucky applicants whose certificates have expired may be admitted but may enroll for only six hours before applying to EPSB for a re-issued certificate. After six hours, a reissued certificate must be submitted before additional registration. Out-of-state applicants with expired certificates must renew in their respective states.

Please refer to the admission section of the WKU graduate catalog for Graduate Studies admission requirements.
