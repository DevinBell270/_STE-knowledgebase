# Middle Level Education in Social Studies and Language Arts, Bachelor of Science (Ref: 5001)

*School of Teacher Education*  
*College of Education and Behavioral Sciences*  
*Western Kentucky University*

The **Bachelor of Science in Middle Level Education in Social Studies and Language Arts (Reference # 5001)** leads to certification for **grades 5–9** in Social Studies, Language Arts / Communication, or both.

The catalog requires:
1. **44–47 semester hours of professional education courses:** EDU 250, EDU 260, PSY 310, EDU 350, PSY 422, EDU 360, and LME 448; MGE 395, MGE 396, plus a 3-hour MGE methods course for each area of certification, MGE 490, and EDU 489; and
2. The indicated content-area coursework for dual-area or single-area teacher certification in one of three concentrations.

**Concentrations:** Social Studies and Language Arts/Communication — dual certification (MESD); Social Studies — single certification (MESS); Language Arts/Communication — single certification (MELA).

Program Coordinator (catalog): [[people/dr-janet-tassell|Janet L. Tassell]] ([janet.tassell@wku.edu](mailto:janet.tassell@wku.edu), 270-745-5306).

Student teaching prerequisites (catalog): all professional education and content courses with grades of **C** or higher; at least a **2.75 GPA** overall, in professional education, and in each content area; specified ratings on dispositional and critical performance assessments. Students with this major receive a **12-hour waiver** in the upper-division hour requirement in the major field.

*   **PSY 310:** Educational Psychology: Development and Learning (3)
*   **EDU 350:** Student Diversity and Differentiation (3)
*   **PSY 422:** Adolescent Psychology (3)

## Concentration 1: Dual Area Certification (MESD) (58–61 Hours)

### Methods
*   **MGE 475:** Teaching Language Arts (3)
*   **MGE 481:** Clinical Practices in Secondary Teaching II: Social Studies (4)

### Social Studies (dual area, 27–30 hours)
*   **HIST 101:** World History I (3)
*   **HIST 102:** World History II (3)
*   **HIST 240:** The United States to 1865 (3)
*   **HIST 241:** The United States Since 1865 (3)
*   **GEOG 110:** World Regional Geography **or** **GEOG 330:** Introduction to Cultural Geography (3)
*   **PS 110:** American National Government (3)
*   **Select one:** ECON 150 Introduction to Economics (3) **or** ECON 202 & ECON 203 Principles of Economics (Micro) and (Macro) (3–6)
*   **Select a 3-hour elective examining ancient to early modern history** from: HIST 304, 305, 306, 307, 308, 310, 317, 318, 323, 330, 364, 398, 404, 407, 419, 440, 460, 463
*   **Select a 3-hour elective examining U.S. history to 1877** from: HIST 325, 353, 358, 390, 441, 442, 443, 445, 457, 481

### Language Arts/Communication (dual area, 24 hours)
*   **ENG 100:** Introduction to College Writing (3)
*   **ENG 300:** Writing in the Disciplines (3)
*   **ENG 302:** Language and Communication (3)
*   **ENG 391:** Survey of American Literature I **or** **ENG 392:** Survey of American Literature II (3)
*   **COMM 145:** Fundamentals of Public Speaking and Communication (3)
*   **LME 318:** Children's Literature (3)
*   **Select two of the following (6):** ENG 301 Argument and Analysis in Written Discourse (recommended); ENG 401 Advanced Composition (an option, but ENG 301 and 410 are preferable); ENG 410 Composition Theory and Practice in Writing Instruction (recommended)

## Concentration 2: Social Studies Single Certification (MESS) (40–43 Hours)

*   **MGE 481:** Clinical Practices in Secondary Teaching II: Social Studies (4)
*   Social Studies content (36–39 hours): HIST 101, HIST 102, HIST 240, HIST 241, GEOG 110 or GEOG 330, PS 110, ECON 150 **or** ECON 202 & 203 (3–6), plus **15 hours** of electives from the catalog list (HIST 304, 305, 306, 307, 308, 310, 311, 317, 318, 323, 325, 330, 353, 358, 364, 375, 390, 398, 402, 404, 407, 419, 440, 441, 442, 443, 445, 453, 454, 457, 460, 463, 481; GEOG 330, GEOG 352; PS 310, PS 326, PS 435).

Catalog footnote: at least **twelve hours** must be in History, three of which must examine histories outside the United States. At least one of these courses must be a non-U.S., non-European history course.
