# Clinical Experiences & Student Teaching

*Office of Professional Educator Services (OPES)*  
*School of Teacher Education*  
*Western Kentucky University*

Clinical preparation is the cornerstone of educator preparation at Western Kentucky University. Teacher candidates engage in extensive, structured, and diverse field experiences that systematically connect university coursework to authentic classroom practice.

---

## The 200-Hour Pre-Student Teaching Requirement

In compliance with Kentucky Administrative Regulation (16 KAR 5:040), all undergraduate and traditional graduate initial certification candidates must complete at least **200 clock hours of diverse field experiences** prior to admission to student teaching.

### Required Categories of Field Experience:
Candidates must document clinical experiences across seven state-mandated categories:
1.  **Direct Observation & Interaction:** Engaging with students in primary, middle, and secondary classrooms.
2.  **Student Diversity:** Working with diverse student populations, including students from varying socioeconomic, ethnic, racial, and linguistic backgrounds.
3.  **Special Education & Gifted Education:** Observing and instructing students with disabilities (IEPs/504 plans) and gifted/talented students.
4.  **English Learners (EL):** Tutoring and working with multilingual students.
5.  **Family & Community Engagement:** Participating in parent-teacher conferences, school open houses, family literacy nights, and school board meetings.
6.  **School Operational Contexts:** Attending site-based decision-making (SBDM) council meetings and professional learning communities (PLCs).
7.  **Service-Learning & Tutoring:** Conducting one-on-one and small-group academic remediation.

All 200 hours are logged and verified through Kentucky's state electronic tracking system (KEC / KFETS) and audited by OPES in GRH 1092. See [[opes/field-experience|OPES Field Experience Procedures]] for complete visitation guidelines.

## The Culminating Student Teaching Semester

Student teaching is the capstone clinical experience—a **full-time, 16-week (70 instructional days) immersive placement** in an accredited P-12 public school.

### Key Operational Features:
*   **Watermark SL&L Application:** Applications are submitted one full year in advance through the **Watermark Student Learning & Licensure** platform (deadlines: **October 15** for Fall, **April 15** for Spring). Managed by Program Manager [[people/shannon-evans|Shannon Evans]] (see [[opes/student-teaching-procedures|Student Teaching Procedures]]).
*   **The WKU Co-Teaching Model:** WKU utilizes an evidence-based co-teaching framework developed in collaboration with [[people/dr-rebecca-stobaugh|Dr. Rebecca Stobaugh]]. Mentor cooperating teachers complete state-mandated 16 KAR 5:040 training (Parts A, B, and C) and earn graduate tuition waivers (see [[opes/co-teaching-model|WKU Co-Teaching Model]]).
*   **Gradual Release of Responsibility:** The candidate systematically transitions from assisting to leading instruction, culminating in a multi-week period of solo teaching.
*   **Clinical Supervision:** Every candidate is observed, coached, and evaluated on multiple site visits by a WKU university clinical supervisor (e.g., [[people/katie-decker|Katie Decker]], [[people/kim-taylor|Kim Taylor]]).
*   **Seminar Capstone (EDU 489):** Weekly on-campus reflective seminar addressing legal issues, trauma-informed practices, classroom management crises, and job search interview preparation.
