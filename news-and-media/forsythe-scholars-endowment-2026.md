# Forsythe Scholars Endowment ($1M) Supports STE Students

*Source coverage: The Lane Report; College Heights Herald (May 27, 2026). Gift announced for late 2024; first awards reported spring 2026.*

## Summary

In late 2024, **Charlotte Forsythe** and **Jerry Fandetti** of Rowley, Massachusetts, made a **$1 million gift** establishing the **Forsythe Scholars Endowment** for students pursuing degrees in the WKU [[overview/about-ste|School of Teacher Education]]. First scholarships from the endowment were awarded for the **2025–2026** academic year to **19 students**. The College Heights Foundation reported the endowment will fund about **$40,000** in scholarships for **2026–2027** and beyond.

## How the endowment can be used

Overseen by the Dean of the [[overview/leadership-and-governance|College of Education & Behavioral Sciences]], funds may support:

* Undergraduate and graduate tuition scholarships within STE
* Emergency student assistance
* Opportunities outside the classroom (e.g., professional conferences)
* Classroom supplies for student teaching
* Other STE student needs

## Notable voices

* **WKU President Timothy C. Caboni** highlighted the donors’ long support of Hilltopper students and the gift’s role in recruiting and retaining future educators.
* **Dr. Corinne Murphy**, CEBS Dean, called the gift a model of donor-informed impact on the education profession.
* **Erin Atwell (’25)**, Elementary Education alum pursuing [[programs/graduate/libraries-informatics-technology-ms-0497|LITE (Ref: 0497)]] while teaching in Warren County Schools, received a tuition scholarship and described it as reducing the stress of graduate study as a first-year teacher.

## Family legacy

The gift honors Forsythe family ties to WKU’s teacher-education lineage (including Ethel Mallory Forsythe’s connection to the Bowling Green Business School / Western Kentucky State Teachers’ College lineage) and prior Forsythe family scholarships.
